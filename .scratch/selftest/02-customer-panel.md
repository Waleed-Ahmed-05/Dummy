# Customer panel: StudyBuddy flashcards (SYNTHETIC SELF-TEST)

Source: `.scratch/selftest/01-brief.md`. Judged on the problem and the offer as written. The planned landing-page claim ("improve their grades by 40%") is the brief's own demand claim, so it was not used as evidence for any verdict.

| Segment | Notice | Return | Pay | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. First-year undergrads cramming for exams | Yes. Exam panic is a real, frequent pain. But they already use free tools: Anki (free on desktop and Android), Quizlet's free tier, paper notes, past papers and class WhatsApp groups. A new email-signup web app has to beat these, and nothing in the brief says how it does. | Unclear, leaning no. Crammers study in short bursts just before an exam, which is the opposite of spaced repetition. They also have to build their own decks, which is extra work exactly when they are short on time. A daily "5 cards due" reminder may bring some back, but only near exams. Coming back on 3+ separate days in 2 weeks only fits if an exam happens to fall in that window. | No. The brief has no price, and free alternatives exist. Students in this segment are price-sensitive. No one else is named as the payer. | Return |
| 2. Tutors who prepare decks for their students | Yes, in principle. Tutors already make revision material and would see a deck tool as relevant. | No, as the offer stands. The brief describes a personal tool only: each student makes and reviews their own decks. It has no way to share or assign a deck to students and no view of student progress. Without those, a tutor has no reason to come back. | Possibly, since it is a business expense for a tutor, or the tutor could pass the cost on to students. But the tutor features that would justify paying are not in the brief. Their current tools (Quizlet sets, printed sheets, WhatsApp) already let them share. | Return |

**Overall verdict: Return.** Neither segment gives a clear yes on Return or Pay for the offer as written.

## What would change the verdict

- **Tutors (closest to a Pass):** Add tutor-to-student deck sharing and a simple per-student "who reviewed what" view. That gives tutors a reason to come back each week and something worth paying for. Their students then get ready-made decks, which also fixes the crammers' deck-building problem.
- **Crammers:** Fit the product to how they actually study. Offer ready-made or importable decks for specific courses, and time the reminders around exam dates. Or aim the 3-day return goal at an exam window and not at any 2 weeks.
- **Either segment:** Say why someone would pick this over Anki or Quizlet, which are free and which both segments already use. Without that reason, "notice" does not turn into a switch.
- **Strategist, one line:** Rebuild the offer around tutors who share decks with their students and can see their students' review progress, so tutors have a reason to return and to pay.
